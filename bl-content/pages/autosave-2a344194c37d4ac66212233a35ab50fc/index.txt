<p>Some years ago, I lived in Cape Town, South Africa, for a year. A very interesting experience that I highly recommend.</p>
<p>To commute to work, I used to take the famous "minibus taxis". This is the most popular transport system across much of Africa and, in my view, the most effective one, too. Let me explain why, and how it offers an interesting analogy for digital accessibility.</p>
<p>While these minibuses have designated stops, the drivers are flexible: if you raise your arm, they’ll stop right where you are, and the same to get off, just ask the driver nicely where you want to be dropped off. The routes are also set, but with room for "flexible detours", as drivers can adjust the path based on traffic or at a passenger’s request. The same applies to schedules: during rush hour, they run very frequently, while at other times, you might have to wait a bit.</p>
<p>Digital accessibility is, in many ways, just like this. Flexibility and adaptability are essential. Every user has different preferences, abilities, and circumstances. A point at the heart of WCAG. Our products must adapt to various technologies, the different ways users complete tasks, how individuals process information, and everyone's personal preferences.</p>
<p>By the way, the capacity of these minibuses is also adaptable. There’s always room for one more passenger! But that’s a topic for another time.</p>
